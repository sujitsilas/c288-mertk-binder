Targeting MerTK to improve efferocytosis

Efferocytosis is the removal of dying cells by immune cells. I designed proteins to help macrophages use MerTK for uptake and to protect the receptor from ADAM17-mediated cleavage during inflammation. MerTK shedding study

    Planned experimental validation

    The next experiments test receptor binding, function, and shedding directly.

    Confirm binding and GAS6 compatibility

    Use biolayer interferometry with mammalian-expressed MerTK to measure affinity and GAS6 binding in both addition orders. Prioritize at least twofold affinity over AXL and TYRO3 and at least 80% of the GAS6-only binding signal.

    Aim 1 · Efferocytosis in the mouse burn-wound model

    Confirm mouse MerTK binding before treatment. Compare the lead construct with vehicle and a matched nonbinding control. Quantify apoptotic neutrophils engulfed by wound macrophages and track closure and re-epithelialization. Test receptor dependence under MerTK blockade.

    Aim 2 · Test protection after shedding begins

    Compare wild-type and cleavage-resistant human MerTK at matched surface levels. If cleavage resistance preserves uptake, test an unmasked protector in stimulated macrophages. Measure released MerTK by ELISA, surface MerTK by flow cytometry, and GAS6-induced MerTK phosphorylation.
