## The decision and its biological value

Design a **monovalent, human MERTK-binding probe** and test whether it preserves GAS6 and PROS1 binding. The project asks one question: can we add a specific experimental handle to an extracellular receptor surface without obstructing native ligand recognition? The primary biological result is specific receptor binding with biochemical ligand compatibility. Useful receptor activation and improved efferocytosis would need later evidence.

This is an enabling reagent project. A ligand-compatible probe could support later receptor labeling or controlled valency studies, but those derivatives are not required deliverables. A binding hit that blocks either ligand fails the present biological objective, while remaining an informative negative result.

The previous proposal bundled discovery, receptor agonism, shedding protection, a new PS bridge, method benchmarking, cross-species engineering and tissue efficacy. Each adds an independent failure mode. Published MER-activating antibodies have both enhanced uptake in a screen ([Santulli-Marotto et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0145078)) and impaired efferocytosis in another disease context ([Waterborg et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2018.00742)). Binding, phosphorylation and clearance are therefore distinct claims.

| Candidate class project | What must work | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Monovalent, ligand-compatible minibinder | Native receptor access, binding, ligand compatibility | Chosen: one new binding interface, measurable endpoints and a small protein panel |
| De novo MERTK agonist | Binding plus productive membrane receptor organization and useful uptake | Defer until there is a characterized binding parent |
| Shedding-protective binder | Binding plus protection of a species-specific cleavage-sensitive region, without receptor depletion | Defer: human cleavage geometry is absent from 37NT, and neither patch has protection evidence |
| Annexin A5–PROS1 LG fusion | Two functional modules, correct processing, suitable spacing, simultaneous PS/receptor engagement and productive signaling | Defer: attractive mechanism, but several coupled structural and biological uncertainties |
| Large generator benchmark with mouse/tissue extension | Multiple reliable runtimes, a large enough comparison, cross-species binding and downstream biology | Defer; a small RFD4 secondary arm can contribute candidates to the same biological question |

## Why this receptor surface and these tools?

Experimental human 37NT supplies the folded receptor reference. Its observed A93-A484 segment includes Ig and FNIII domains with partial insect-cell glycans; it does not include the complete stalk, membrane or kinase. Native GAS6/PROS1 recognition involves the Ig region, so FNIIIa provides a reasonable **hypothesis** for ligand-compatible binding. It is not evidence of a harmless epitope or a solved natural-ligand footprint ([Starbird et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1042/BCJ20260584); [Lew et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.03385)).

Patch A's I365/W367/A369 anchors are exposed in our coordinate analysis, but the nearest observed glycan is only 8.6 Å from W367. Inspect a whole proposed binder footprint against all observed glycans, unresolved glycosylation sites and adjoining domains before freezing an epitope. Preserve native target residues. Reject a pose that exploits a crop edge or requires removing a glycan. Mammalian glycoforms remain an uncertainty to resolve with mammalian-produced receptor and cell-surface binding. At the user's request, a separately recorded Ig1 K178/E183/V185 pilot now tests a second surface. It is an explicit epitope comparison, not an undisclosed rescue of Patch A. Its site and ligand-overlay limitations are documented in the amendment. Both sites' failures remain in the attempt record.

Use BindCraft as the primary generator, with its upstream sequence-design/filtering steps. Its published binder results justify a trial, not a promised MERTK hit ([Pacesa et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09429-6)). The installed environment has passed a limited A100 runtime check; the complete MERTK design workflow remains unverified. The user requested retaining RFD4, so it contributes a **bounded secondary arm** on the same crop, binder length and provisional anchors. One upstream PPI control must pass the raw-output geometry screen before two new MERTK replicates may run. If the control fails, retain the diagnostic and continue the BindCraft arm; do not let debugging expand into another class aim. This small, unequal-budget comparison cannot establish method superiority. Separate LigandMPNN refinement and custom model training add no necessary test of the selected question. AF3 is a computational cross-check, not an experimental binding assay.

## A bounded campaign and a declared nomination rule

These are **new campaign settings**. The historical `pilot_20261110_01` files and locked assessment protocol remain unchanged. The first tranche is prepared separately as `course_20261112_patchA_01`, with input and workflow hashes, residue maps, pinned tool revisions and recorded scheduler commands. Do not invoke the old two-arm cohort preparer as though it implements the new cropped-target/native-context nomination plan.

- Use an 80-residue monovalent binder at FNIIIa Patch A or the separately recorded exploratory Ig1 side surface. Design crops retain the complete observed FNIII pair, A286-A484, or Ig pair/linker, A93-A285, respectively; restore every candidate against the entire A93-A484 reference before nomination. The two fresh campaigns together reserve at most 14 A100-hours within the unchanged shared cap.
- Stop after **20 attempted BindCraft trajectories or 24 allocated A100-hours across both generators, the RFD4 control and AF3, whichever comes first**. The compute cap is a resource limit, not a runtime prediction or guarantee of 20 completed trajectories. The initial tranche caps BindCraft at two trajectories/four hours, the RFD4 control at one replicate/one hour, and RFD4 MERTK at two replicates/two hours: at most seven allocated A100-hours. Record early terminations, completed trajectories, seeds, GPU time and every rejection. Limit BindCraft sequence nomination to two variants per trajectory: at most 40 sequence proposals for the full primary arm, not 40 independent backbones. Secondary RFD4 MERTK replicates use seed 202, batch size one and the upstream medium/front-loaded schedule; the upstream PD-L1 control uses seed 101 and its original 50-120-residue binder range.
- Preserve native BindCraft outputs and filters; do not weaken them to fill the panel. Advance at most four provisional candidates from distinct trajectories, selected for sequence/interface diversity, geometry and intact-context access.
- Run AF3 on the complete observed receptor plus each binder, using the same declared single-sequence/no-template inputs, seeds 101/202/303, five samples per seed and the predeclared top-ranked representative per seed. This deliberately bounded assessment does not use a target MSA or reconstruct a glycosylated membrane receptor.
- Require all four criteria in at least two of three seed representatives: mean binder atom pLDDT at least 80; MERTK–binder pair ipTM at least 0.7; median bidirectional interface PAE at most 10 Å; binder C-alpha RMSD to the designed pose at most 3 Å after alignment on the complete intended domain (FNIIIa A286-A381 or Ig1 A93-A186). If pair scores are asymmetric, use the lower direction. Define interface residue sets from the proposed design's protein heavy-atom contacts within 5 Å, before inspecting AF3 outputs; use the corresponding two cross-chain PAE blocks. Report missing interface/score data as unevaluable rather than a pass.
- Independently inspect local backbone geometry and per-domain preservation against 37NT, observed glycan overlap, unresolved-sequon risk, exposed hydrophobicity and unsatisfied buried polar groups. A global ectodomain RMSD alone cannot distinguish domain movement from local distortion. Require the campaign's intended epitope; an apparently confident alternative pose is not recovery of the design.

These thresholds are screening heuristics selected for this course proposal, not validated MERTK affinity cutoffs. Up to four finalists means **zero to four across both arms**, with no substitute designs added after seeing wet-lab outcomes. Pair ipTM, interface PAE and pose recovery must be reported separately. The initial jobs implement input validation and preliminary raw geometry/native-context screening. They do not implement every AF3 nomination metric above; those checks and glycan-footprint review precede any AF3 submission. AF3 is not automatically queued without usable candidates, and failure of one arm need not block assessment of a useful candidate from the other.

## What constitutes an objective result?

| Level | Required evidence | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Course computational deliverable | Reviewed epitope dossier, fixed-budget attempt accounting, all structures/scores, nomination/rejection reasons, synthesis/control package for any passing candidates | Computationally nominated candidates, or no qualifying candidate under this specification and budget |
| Binding success | Soluble, monomeric candidate; reproducible concentration-dependent binding to mammalian-produced MERTK and full-length cell-surface receptor; negative controls | MERTK binding; proposed affinity goal ≤100 nM and ≥10-fold biochemical selectivity over each human TAM off-target |
| Main biological success | Both GAS6 and PROS1 binding preserved during candidate occupancy, with interpretable controls and uncertainty | Biochemical ligand compatibility; proposed maximum tolerated loss 20% |
| Optional functional evidence | Best hit tested in paired primary-human macrophage/neutrophil uptake with a MERTK pathway control and extracellular-signal exclusion | Preliminary uptake effect, with donor-level uncertainty; no mouse, resolution or regeneration claim |

For competition, test both addition orders at near-saturating candidate occupancy and ligand near its measured binding midpoint. Start with three independent assay runs/preparations with paired controls; technical repeats estimate assay noise rather than adding independent samples. Specify the analysis before testing: background-corrected ligand signal with candidate divided by matched ligand-alone signal, and a 95% confidence interval for the ratio, for each ligand separately. A compatibility claim requires the lower bound to support a ratio of at least 0.80 for both ligands. This proposed margin is a design decision, not a universal biological threshold. An inconclusive interval is not proof of compatibility; do not repeat selectively until it passes.

The optional three-donor uptake experiment estimates effects and variability; it is not a powered noninferiority or efficacy study. It follows one passing hit and one ligand-supported condition, rather than expanding to a donor-by-ligand-by-valency screen. If it shows reduced uptake, keep only the biochemical binding/compatibility labels and investigate the discrepancy in later work.

If experimental facilities or time are unavailable, the computational package and falsifiable validation plan remain the course output. They must not be called a validated binder. Even with wet-lab access, no positive biological outcome is guaranteed. The achievable commitment is a finite, interpretable experiment with a clear endpoint and honest negative results.
