## What ADAM17 uses

ADAM17 is a zinc metalloprotease. Its catalytic domain cuts the extracellular juxtamembrane stalk, disconnecting the ligand-binding region from the membrane/kinase portion. The human cleavage bond is reported as P490–S491 in the 2026 nanobody study. The classic direct cleavage mapping and six-residue deletion work used mouse P485–S486; do not transfer mouse residue numbers to human constructs. The original paper emphasizes relaxed sequence specificity and the importance of stalk length/accessibility. A design that recognizes only a short consensus sequence is therefore less justified than one that physically shields the accessible stalk. [Thorp 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3190938/), [Duprez 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13378718/).

ADAM17 also uses membrane interactions: exposed phosphatidylserine (PS) binds its membrane-proximal domain and supports shedding in the studied substrate systems. This is not a mapped MERTK-specific docking interface. CANDIS can participate in substrate recognition, demonstrated for IL-6R/IL-1RII, but those results do not establish a MERTK exosite. Broad PS masking could interfere with apoptotic-cell recognition, directly conflicting with this project's efferocytosis objective. Distinguish PS on the phagocyte carrying MERTK/ADAM17 from PS on the apoptotic cargo. [Sommer 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4866515/), [Düsterhöft 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4047402/).

## Ranked preservation strategies

| Priority | Architecture | Rationale and unresolved question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soluble monovalent MERTK protector | The strongest direct precedent is MERTK-binding nanobodies that reduce shedding while preserving GAS6 binding. Exact steric versus allosteric protection is unresolved; binding to Ig1 alone cannot be assumed to protect the distant stalk. |
| 2 | Membrane-tethered protector on the **same phagocyte** | Express a protector outside the cell on an independent single-pass membrane anchor, or explore a GPI anchor; a lipid-conjugated protector is a delivery alternative. Tethering could raise local concentration, but orientation, receptor proximity, anchor distribution and internalization must be measured. The anchor and linker must themselves resist shedding. No existing project binder is ready for this format. |
| 3 | Biparatopic Ig binder + stalk-shielding module | Ig engagement could supply receptor specificity and a second module could cover the cleavage region. Large geometric separation and uncertain stalk orientation make this a new architecture, not a short-linker fusion of current sequences. Start with monovalent receptor occupancy to distinguish protection from crosslink-driven trafficking. |
| 4 | MERTK-localized ADAM17 inhibitor | A MERTK-binding arm could concentrate an inhibitory module nearby. Inference: localization may improve selectivity, but enzyme inhibition could still affect neighboring substrates. Needs a validated inhibitor module and assays of other ADAM17 substrates; no MERTK-specific enzyme-contact surface has been established here. |
| Control / alternative for engineered cells | Cleavage-resistant MERTK stalk | Strong causal comparator in mouse work. Human substitutions/deletions require species mapping and validation of expression, ligand response and efferocytosis. This changes the receptor and is not a soluble-binder treatment. |

Direct MERTK-binding anti-shedding nanobodies provide the relevant benchmark. Their bivalent format activated signaling and reduced surface MERTK, so dimerization and preservation must be assessed separately. Monovalent protection should come first. [Duprez 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13378718/). Cleavage-resistant mouse receptors improved efferocytosis/resolution in disease models, supporting preservation as a biological aim. [Cai 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5272169/).

## What membrane anchoring can and cannot accomplish

MERTK already has a transmembrane helix. Adding a lipid/GPI tether to its released ectodomain could retain ligand-binding material at the surface, but does not reconnect it to the original intracellular kinase. Retention might even retain a ligand decoy. A separately anchored **protector**, acting before cleavage, is the more coherent use of an anchor. GPI replacement of MERTK's transmembrane/kinase region would remove its normal signaling machinery.

A receptor-bound Ig1 binder is already localized through MERTK occupancy; membrane tethering is useful only if it improves residence, orientation or shielding. In the AFDB full-length model, the Ig1 site centroid is about 153 Å from the cleavage bond and Ig2 about 123 Å. These distances are model-dependent: stalk pLDDT is only ~33–40 and interdomain orientation is uncertain. They argue against assuming a compact Ig-bound binder directly covers P490–S491; they are not linker-length prescriptions. ## Discriminating experiments

Compare soluble and tethered monovalent protector, anchor-only and binding-defective formats, and a justified cleavage-resistant receptor comparator. Read reduced soluble MERTK together with intact surface MERTK and cleavage fragments using detection epitopes unaffected by the binder. Measure GAS6/PROS1 responsiveness, receptor phosphorylation/internalization and actual apoptotic-cell internalization, not attachment alone. Verify MERTK dependence and distinguish extracellular cleavage inhibition from altered expression/trafficking. Include a broad ADAM17 inhibitor as a mechanistic control and monitor a second ADAM17 substrate to detect general protease suppression. A soluble peptide-cleavage assay alone does not reproduce membrane substrate recognition.

A GPI, lipid or single-pass anchor has not been selected or designed in this triage. These are ranked hypotheses. The immediate executable question is whether the existing Ig1 geometry can be repaired; successful repair still requires independent refolding, ligand compatibility and function checks.
