# Choosing a protease trigger for a MERTK shedding protector

November 18, 2026

## Pooled transcript counts used in the inflammation proposal

I combined all ten cell types and both conditions across the 21 mouse samples. Of 547 annotated protease-panel genes present in the feature list, 545 had at least one transcript. Ctsb ranked first (500,315 counts), followed by Asprv1 (442,271), Ctss (378,282) and Adam8 (307,652). Mmp12 ranked sixth overall (211,501) and first among the matrix metalloproteinases. Adam17 ranked 44th (51,413) and Mmp8 ranked 86th (32,349). Ctsb also ranked first when each sample contributed equally by mean counts per million. Mmp12 ranked fifth under that weighting. The cohort profiles RNA abundance in one tissue dataset. The proposal tests protease activity in independent human inflammatory models.

The H5AD has an empty layers group and no raw slot, but its two library-size columns permit recovery of gene-level integer counts. For each cell, multiply `expm1(X)` by `nCount_originalexp / 10000`, apply `expm1` again, and multiply by `nCount_RNA / 10000` before rounding. Across all 136,625,904 nonzero entries, the greatest distance from an integer was 0.226 counts or less. The reconstructed profiles matched the recorded library size and number of detected genes in every one of 77,967 cells and totaled 356,927,606 counts. Applying the two forward transforms reproduced X with maximum absolute error below 0.00000043. Zeros remained zero. No raw gene-count matrix was supplied separately.

The rank table includes pooled transcript counts, percentage of cells with a detected transcript, total samples and cell types with detection, pooled CPM, and mean CPM with each sample given equal weight. Pooled counts use all cells including those from small cell-type groups. The earlier differential-expression analysis below used only the outer inverse normalization and is retained for provenance. Its coefficients describe the original transformed analysis and are not used as transcript counts or as a selection criterion in this proposal.

## Earlier burn–sham comparison

Mmp8 is the first extracellular protease to investigate as an inflammation-responsive trigger. In the annotated Mono/MDM population, its mean normalized expression was 3.14 in burn versus 1.05 in sham per 10,000. The day-adjusted difference on the log2 scale was +1.54 (95% interval +1.05 to +2.04, panel q = 0.0053). The effect stayed positive when omitting any one of the three informative days (+1.10 to +2.15). The mean across all cells within each sample also increased (+1.39, q = 0.0166). Cell composition can contribute to that pooled result. No protease increased at q < 0.05 in every cell type. Enzyme release, zymogen activation, inhibitors and surface accessibility all intervene between RNA abundance and extracellular catalysis.

Protease transcripts alone cannot identify the primary cellular source of an active enzyme in wound fluid. Macrophage Mertk and Gas6 both trended lower (transcriptome q = 0.086 and 0.099), adding a reason to measure intact surface receptor, available ligand and receptor cleavage independently.

## Linker selection and mechanism

Use **GPQG↓IWGQ** as the first cleavable linker core to characterize. Netzel-Arnett and colleagues measured a catalytic efficiency of 40 µM⁻¹ h⁻¹ for purified human neutrophil collagenase (MMP8) on the peptide with this core, versus 5.7 for the related GPQG↓IAGQ sequence in the same assay. Human fibroblast collagenase (MMP1) also cleaved the W-containing substrate. These data provide a matched slower comparator and a documented cross-reactivity control. The published measurements were made on assay peptides. The folded fusion, species and extracellular environment can all change the rate. Mouse burn expression prioritizes the trigger for human testing. [Netzel-Arnett et al., Table III](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1849891/).

| Design or control | What would make it informative? |
|---|---|
| Unmasked, monovalent anti-shedding binder | Establish the amount of shedding reduction and GAS6 binding the active protector can deliver. Duprez et al. describe A0445046C08 and its half-life-extended derivative A044500050 as relevant benchmarks. |
| Protector with a removable mask over its own binding surface | A cut in the W-containing core releases the mask and exposes the protector. Measure the time to regain MERTK binding and compare it with the time course of shedding. The exposed protector must still reach native receptor. |
| Matched slow and independently verified cleavage-resistant fusions | Estimate whether changing the rate of activation alters shedding protection at matched protein exposure. Confirm basal binding, expression and stability to interpret differences. |
| Cleaved protector, linker alone, receptor-localized bait without a protective interface | Identify any transient benefit of substrate competition and whether durable protection requires the anti-shedding binder. Measure receptor cleavage both before and after the bait has been cut. |
| Each Ig1 parent alone | Detect any indirect effect on shedding, receptor trafficking or GAS6 binding even when the bound pose cannot physically cover the stalk. |

A single sacrificial cut leaves the protease available for subsequent turnovers. In particular, an MMP8 substrate provides no direct mechanism for consuming ADAM17. Protection would require receptor occupancy that limits access to the stalk, an allosteric effect, or competitive diversion shown at relevant concentrations. A permanently bound monovalent protector supplies the simplest benchmark. Shin et al. demonstrated restored signaling after MMP2 removed a mask from a cytokine mimic. Their transferable result is the order of activation. They did not establish MERTK shedding protection. An independent study also tested protease-removable masks on designed miniprotein binders. [Shin et al., Fig. 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42818144/) · [Protease-activatable minibinders](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41284265/) · [Duprez et al. on MERTK](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42459154/) · [Thorp et al. on ADAM17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21828049/).

First test the actual fusion against active human MMP8, MMP1, MMP9, MMP12 and ADAM17. Test matched species enzymes or independent mouse burn/sham extracellular specimens before attributing cleavage in mouse material to MMP8. Quantify intact fusion and cleavage products over time, with a verified enzyme activity control, a matched protease-depleted or inhibited condition and precleaved fusion. A strong fluorescence change alone can reflect cleavage at another bond, so identify the product. Then test intact surface MERTK, released ectodomain, GAS6 binding, GAS6-supported phosphorylation and apoptotic-cell internalization in stimulated human macrophages. Measure MERTK dependence, viability and a second ADAM17 substrate. Select the cleavable version only if activation in burn material is reproducible, timely and preserves receptor function. A stable protector is the preferred comparator if extracellular cleavage is similar in sham and burn.

## What Hoffman2 tested

CPU job **2084470** completed with exit code 0 on n1183 in four seconds. Using the existing experimental Ig1 binding frames for mpnn12 and mpnn8, the job superimposed 94 membrane-proximal Cα positions from each of three related stalk models onto human MERTK structure 37NT (alignment RMSD 1.46 Å). It then measured distance from each binder to the midpoint of the modeled P490–S491 peptide bond. The original mouse study mapped P485–S486. [Human shedding study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42459154/) · [Mouse cleavage mapping](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21828049/).

| Existing Ig1 parent | Nearest binder atom to modeled cut | N terminus to cut | C terminus to cut |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mpnn12 | 118.9–119.2 Å | 157.6–157.8 Å | 159.0–159.3 Å |
| mpnn8 | 119.2–119.4 Å | 158.1–158.4 Å | 159.7–160.0 Å |

The models perturb local stalk geometry and share a common global receptor frame. The stalk after residue 484 has low prediction confidence. As an independent check on stalk coordinates, even the nearest binder atom is 113.2–113.7 Å from the **observed** residue 484 Cα. Allowing the missing segment a generous maximum reach of 26.4 Å leaves at least 86.8 Å to the cut in that receptor frame. The eight-residue core has at most about 35 Å of contour between flanking attachments. This rules out direct coverage by the current poses plus this short peptide. Large changes in receptor-domain orientation, a separate protective module and indirect changes in shedding are different hypotheses. The measurements do not predict ligand compatibility or catalytic rates.

The existing Ig1 parents belong to one E152 backbone and remain candidates for native-geometry repair. Ig2 has no accepted candidate. The two prior stalk-generation pilots rejected all 280 sequence variants at the base AF2 stage. No new mask sequence was generated by the geometry job. ## Analysis and reproduction

- The supplied mouse-gene-symbol dataset contains 77,967 cells, 20,426 features and 21 sample IDs. Burn and sham sample counts per day were 3/3 on D7, 3/2 on D10, 3/3 on D14 and 2/2 on D19. The analysis assumes each annotated sample is an independent replicate. Distinct animal identifiers or repeated sampling could change the variance model and require the study sample sheet.
- For this earlier analysis, inverting the outer log1p transformation of X gave a per-cell sum of 10,000 on an intermediate expression scale. For each sample and cell type, average the linear values, transform with log2(mean + 0.1), then fit a day-adjusted limma model with robust, trend-aware empirical Bayes. A positive log2 coefficient denotes higher burn expression. The small-expression offset matters for low-abundance genes. The new pooled analysis above reverses both transformations to recover integer counts.
- Retain sample–cell-type groups with at least 20 cells, days represented in both arms, and cell types with at least three samples per arm across retained days. Require a gene to be detected in at least three cells and 1% of cells in at least three eligible samples. All ten types passed. Mono/MDM retained D7, D10 and D14 (9 burn, 8 sham). Sebocytes retained only D14 and D19 (3 per arm). A separate pooled sample model captures within-cell-type and cell-mixture changes.
- Use the reviewed mouse UniProt GO:0008233 peptidase-activity query as a broad panel. The downloaded snapshot has 573 primary gene symbols, of which 479 passed expression filters in at least one cell type. The annotation spans intracellular proteins and proteins associated with proteolytic complexes. Individual extracellular catalytic activity requires separate evidence. Report Benjamini–Hochberg q across all 3,325 tested gene-by-cell-type contrasts and separately across 391 pooled proteases. The full table also gives genome-wide q within each cell type. [UniProt query](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb?query=%28organism_id%3A10090%29%20AND%20%28reviewed%3Atrue%29%20AND%20%28go%3A0008233%29).
- K-means uses six groups, 100 starts and seed 20261118 on the unscaled day-adjusted log2 effects across ten types. Untested effects take a neutral zero solely for the distance calculation and appear gray in the figures. Sparse genes may cluster together because of missing coverage. Colors saturate at ±2 for the contrast plot. Unclipped effects, cluster membership and number of tested types are available in the table. Condition-abundance panels show unadjusted, equally sample-weighted means, in the same gene order.
- From the project root run `Rscript scripts/burn_sham/pseudobulk.R`, followed by `Rscript scripts/burn_sham/figures.R`. The first command reuses the downloaded panel snapshot; a fresh output directory downloads the current UniProt annotation. To generate the current report counts, run `Rscript scripts/burn_sham/pooled_transcripts.R` after the annotation snapshot is available. Re-render the pooled-count figure with `.venv/bin/python scripts/burn_sham/report_figure.py`. The Slurm submission script and staged structural inputs are preserved in the project.
